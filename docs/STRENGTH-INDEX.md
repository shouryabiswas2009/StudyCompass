# The Unicelerate strength index

## How it works and why

**The problem.** Cards had a "Ranking" slot that read `qs_ranking`. We
can't use QS, THE, U.S. News or ShanghaiRanking data (their terms don't
allow it: docs/DATA-SOURCES-CONSIDERED.md), so the slot said "Ranking not
available" for almost every school. The only numbers in it were 14
illustrative sample ranks left over from the demo data (MIT "#1"), which
migration_019 removes.

**The answer.** An honest, labelled estimate from open data, computed once
at import time and stored in the database (the site never computes it per
request):

1. **Signals.** Each available figure becomes a **percentile among
   comparable schools** (rank-based, so skewed figures like earnings need no
   log scale: a percentile is the same either way):
   - College Scorecard (US): graduation rate, first-year retention, median
     earnings 10 years after entry, SAT midpoint, Carnegie research level;
     percentiles among the US schools that report each one.
   - Leiden Ranking Open Edition: research impact, already a percentile
     among its 2,831 universities.
   - OpenAlex: h-index and 2-year mean citedness, averaged, among our
     schools with at least 200 works (fewer, and a couple of papers decide).
   - A ranking entered by hand from its own public page, if any (none yet);
     never the illustrative ones.
2. **Index** = weighted average of the percentiles a school has (0–100);
   missing signals drop out and the rest are re-weighted. Weights, with the
   reasons, are in `lib/strength-config.ts`:
   graduation 25, earnings 20, SAT 15, retention 10, Leiden 15, OpenAlex 10,
   Carnegie 5, verified ranking 15.
   **Teaching colleges:** for US schools the Scorecard outcomes carry 70 of
   100, research 30, so a strong liberal-arts college (Williams, Amherst,
   Pomona…) lands in tier A even though it publishes little.
   **Outside the US** only research figures exist, so there the index is
   research-based; the breakdown on each page says which signals were used.
3. **Estimates.** A school with no signal, or only one weak one, gets the
   **average index of similar schools** (same country and type; if fewer
   than 5, same country; then same type; then all), shown only as the
   peers' middle half ("est. 45–58", "about #300–#700"), marked
   "Estimated from similar schools", always Low confidence.
4. **Confidence** from independent signals (Leiden and OpenAlex count as
   one, "research"): High 4+; Medium 2–3, or research impact on its own;
   Low = estimated.
5. **Tier** from where the index falls among all measured schools in our
   list: A top 10%, B next 20%, C next 30%, D next 25%, E bottom 15%.
   **Position** "#N of 1,688" is the order in our list (ties share a number).

Code: `lib/strength.ts` (pure, tested in `lib/strength.test.ts`),
`lib/strength-config.ts`, `scripts/strength/` (OpenAlex download and the
import), display in `lib/strength-display.ts` and
`components/universities/strength.tsx`.

## Where it's used

- Every card's slot ("Strength"), the university page (tile and full
  breakdown), compare (a row; "best" only when no school is an estimate),
  offers (the "Strength (Unicelerate index)" slider and summary) and the
  "Strongest first (Unicelerate index)" sort.
- **Quality** in "Best you can get into" (`lib/quality.ts`): the index, with
  research impact swapped for the student's field when the school has a
  Leiden figure for it (same weights). A student's own school, which has no
  index, is scored from the figures they entered, as before.
- Every place says it's our own estimate, not a QS/THE-style ranking, and
  "How is this calculated?" lists the signals, sources and licences.

## Checks and numbers

`data/strength/report.md` (regenerated by every build): coverage by country
and confidence, the featured schools with low confidence (to fill by hand
through the curated CSVs), and the Spearman correlation with the Leiden
percentile, including the version **without** the Leiden signal, so the
agreement isn't Leiden agreeing with itself. `scripts/strength/index.test.mjs`
checks the real output: well-known strong universities in tier A, strong
teaching colleges in A or B, no R1 university in E, low graduation mostly in
D/E, every estimate a Low-confidence range.

## Updating it

```bash
npm run data:strength:openalex   # needs OPENALEX_EMAIL; caches raw responses in ml/data/raw
npm run data:strength:build      # index.csv, report.md, supabase/seed_strength/*.sql
```

Review `data/strength/openalex-matches.csv` (low-confidence matches are
never used until accepted in `openalex-overrides.csv`), run the seed files
in the SQL Editor, then `POST /api/revalidate`.
